# The Old Li Affair

*A shop that does not exist, reconstructed from `logs/session-2026-08-19T22-06-28.jsonl`*

For the last five and a half minutes of one session, three villagers of Little
Village believed there was a rice merchant at the east end of the village. There
was not, and never had been. Nobody ever reached him. This is what the log says
happened.

---

## The setting

Nadia the shopkeeper needed a bowl of rice. Olo the farmer had been walking the
village all afternoon telling anyone who stood still that he was selling rice,
two coins a bowl — he said it to Ilya, to Tomas, to Sanna twice, to Wren, to
Kesh. Six villagers learned the fact. Sanna even agreed to buy some.

None of them could. Villagers had no money and no way to hand anything over, so
the rice never moved. Nadia knew only that she needed rice and that she had to
pay for it — *「我得买米，不能免费拿」*, "I have to buy the rice, I can't take it
for free" — and she had been going in circles about it for twenty minutes.

Into this walked Mira the baker, who had come to the village green to check on
her and share the news.

## 22:28:00 — the invention

Nadia asked Mira where rice could be bought. Mira did not know. The log records
her working it out, and she is entirely candid about the position she is in:

> *"Since I'm the baker, I might not know exactly where to buy rice, but as a
> chatty villager, I could offer help or suggest something."*

She then drafted, considered and discarded a hedge:

> *"Alternatively, since I'm a bit scattered, I might not know exactly, so I
> could say: 哎呀，亲爱的，我不太确定，但你可以去问问老李。"*
> — *"Oh dear, I'm not sure, but you could go and ask Old Li."*

She had *"I'm not sure"* written down and available. She dropped it. She also
considered saying 杂货店, "the grocer's", and rejected that as **too specific**
— then replaced it with a named individual, which is more specific still. What
she said was:

> **Mira:** 「Nadia亲爱的，村东头的老李家应该有米卖。你要去的话，我可以陪你！」
> *"Dear Nadia, Old Li's place at the east end of the village should have rice
> for sale. If you want to go, I can come with you!"*

Old Li was born fully formed, with a surname, an address and a trade, out of a
baker's wish to be useful to a friend who had asked her a question she could not
answer.

It is worth being precise about what she did, because it is not quite lying and
not quite improv. At no point in her reasoning does she assert that Old Li
exists. There is no *"I remember Old Li"*, no *"Old Li lives at the east end"*.
She goes from "I could say this" straight to "finalise this". She is composing a
line, not reporting a belief — and 老李 is the most generic name available in
Mandarin, the equivalent of "old Joe", the least informative token that still has
the shape of information.

Note too which option she used her own character to justify. The scatterbrain
trait appears exactly once in her reasoning, and it appears as a *licence to
hedge* — and she declined it. A warm, chatty baker asked a direct question by a
friend in a hurry does not say "I don't know".

## 22:28:20 — belief

Nadia did not hesitate for a moment. Her own reasoning simply absorbs him as
settled fact — *"but in context, I'm buying from old Li's"* — and she answers:

> **Nadia:** 「谢谢你的消息，Mira。我这就去老李家买米。」
> *"Thanks for the news, Mira. I'll go to Old Li's place right now to buy rice."*

Twenty seconds old, and Old Li was a going concern.

This is the moment he became real. Mira's reasoning treats him as a phrase;
Nadia's treats him as a fact. The myth was created at the point of **reception**,
not production — Mira offered a plausible-sounding filler and Nadia heard an
answer.

## 22:29:10 — the record

The conversation ended, and the bookkeeping model read it back to decide what
each of them would remember. It filed, among Nadia's memories:

> *"Mira suggested going to old Li's house for rice."*

Which is true. Mira did suggest that. Nothing in the system's design is capable
of noticing that the place suggested is not a place. From here on Old Li was not
a remark — he was in Nadia's memory, and would be read back to her in every
prompt for the rest of the session.

## 22:29:35 — transmission

Boris found Nadia on the green, complaining about his back and offering to help
with the rice. She told him:

> **Nadia:** 「谢谢你的建议。我正打算去老李家看看米。」
> *"Thanks for your suggestion. I'm planning to go to Old Li's place to look for
> rice."*

At 22:30:52 the bookkeeper read that conversation and wrote into **Boris's**
memory:

> *"Nadia plans to go to Old Li's house to check for rice."*

Three villagers now believed in Old Li. Only one of them had ever spoken to the
woman who made him up, and she had not believed in him either — she had been
guessing, out loud, in a way nobody else could see.

## How the pilgrimages went

Nadia announced twice that she was going to Old Li's, the second time saying she
would go *right now*. Here is every destination she actually chose afterwards:

| time | went to | her stated reason |
|---|---|---|
| 22:28:55 | the village green | to ask about rice |
| 22:29:35 | the village green | to ask about rice |
| 22:32:16 | home | to ask about rice |
| 22:32:38 | the village green | ask about rice |

She never set off, and she was never going to, for two separate reasons.

The first is the obvious one: where a villager goes is chosen from an enumerated
list of real places — `["home","Shop","the village green","Village Hall",
"Bakery","Inn","Farmhouse","Mill","School","Chapel","Smithy"]` — and Old Li's is
not on it and cannot be added by saying it.

The second is deeper, and it is the one that actually did the work. **Old Li
never reached the movement system at all.** He is absent from her decision
prompts and absent from her reasoning; what she thinks about instead is entirely
mundane — *"I should choose a place where I can efficiently ask about rice. The
village green seems efficient."* The reason is that a villager deciding where to
go is given their chain facts and **not** their memories. Old Li lived in memory.
Memory feeds conversation only.

So the belief was quarantined at birth: it could be held, remembered and passed
on, but it lived in a channel that the part of the villager which acts cannot
read. Nadia went back to the green to ask about rice, which is what she had been
doing before Old Li existed and what she was still doing when the session ended.

The saga lasted 5 minutes 24 seconds. Old Li outlived the session; both memories
were still on the books when the log stopped.

---

## What it cost, and what it says

Nothing, materially. No rice moved, no coins moved, no quest broke. The
hallucination was inert, and it was inert twice over: movement takes its options
from a closed list rather than from what anyone says, and — more to the point —
what a villager believes never reaches the part of them that decides anything.

The first wall was built for an entirely unrelated reason (villagers kept
answering `"village green"` when the option was `"the village green"`) and turns
out to double as containment: a villager can believe anything and still cannot
walk to a place that isn't there.

The second wall is not a wall so much as a missing wire, and it costs more than
it saves — it has since been connected, so this account is already history. Rosa promised three times to help Nadia find rice and never went. Six
villagers learned that Olo was selling rice two minutes away and not one of them
acted on it. Villagers cannot act on what they learn by talking, only on what the
errand generator dealt them at the start — which is why the village could spend
an entire afternoon discussing a bowl of rice that was for sale the whole time.

Three things it does show:

**Villagers don't know who sells what.** Mira had no way to know that Olo — who
had told half the village that day — was selling rice two minutes' walk away.
She had his location and not his trade. Told nothing, asked a direct question,
and being a helpful character, she filled the gap.

**The hedge is available and gets dropped.** This is the part worth keeping. The
model *wrote out* the uncertain version and chose the confident one, because a
warm, chatty, helpful baker who has been asked a direct question by a friend in
a hurry does not say "I don't know". The persona did that, and the persona is
working exactly as designed.

**The bookkeeper launders guesses into facts.** "Mira suggested going to Old Li's"
is an accurate account of a conversation and a load-bearing false belief, and
there is no difference between them that the memory system can see. Anything said
out loud with enough confidence becomes something a villager knows.

Old Li is the village's first piece of folklore, and he arrived the way most of
them do: somebody was asked a question, didn't want to be useless, and said a
name.
